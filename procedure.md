## Measurement

### 1. Maximum Length

#### Instrument Required

- Osteometric board

#### Anatomical Landmarks

- **Medial landmark:** most projecting point of the sternal end
- **Lateral landmark:** most projecting point of the acromial end

#### Procedure

1. Place the clavicle on the osteometric board with its inferior surface resting on the board.
2. Position the most medial point of the sternal end firmly against the fixed upright end of the board.
3. Align the clavicle so that it lies straight along the board, despite its natural curvature.
4. Slide the movable upright end of the board until it touches the most lateral projecting point of the acromial end.
5. Read the measurement in millimeters.
6. Record it as Maximum Length of Clavicle.

#### Precautions

- Measure the straight length, not the curved surface.
- Ensure proper alignment of the bone.
- Avoid tilted or oblique positioning.
- Take two or three readings and record the average.

### 2. Middle Shaft Diameter

#### Instrument Required

- Sliding calipers

#### Anatomical Landmarks

- **Point:** mid-point of clavicular shaft
- **Surfaces:** anterior and posterior borders at mid-shaft

#### Procedure

1. Place the clavicle on a flat surface.
2. Identify the mid-point of the clavicular shaft:
   - First determine the maximum length of the clavicle.
   - Mark the exact midpoint of this length.
3. At this midpoint, identify the antero-posterior margins of the shaft.
4. Place the fixed jaw of the sliding caliper on the anterior surface of the clavicle.
5. Place the movable jaw on the posterior surface.
6. Hold the caliper perpendicular to the long axis of the clavicle.
7. Read the measurement in millimeters.
8. Record it as Middle Shaft Diameter of Clavicle.

#### Precautions

- Ensure the midpoint is accurately located.
- Do not take an oblique measurement.
- Avoid excessive pressure with calipers.
- Take two readings and record the average.

### 3. Middle Curvature Angle

#### Instrument Required

- Goniometer

#### Anatomical Landmarks

- **Vertex:** mid-point of clavicular shaft
- **Arms of angle:**
  - Axis of medial half
  - Axis of lateral half

#### Procedure

1. Place the clavicle on a flat horizontal surface with its inferior surface down.
2. Determine the maximum length of the clavicle.
3. Mark the mid-point of the shaft.
4. Identify:
   - the medial half axis of the clavicle (from sternal end to mid-point), and
   - the lateral half axis (from mid-point to acromial end).
5. Draw or imagine straight lines along both halves.
6. Place the centre of the goniometer at the mid-point of the clavicle.
7. Measure the angle formed between the medial and lateral axes.
8. Record the value in degrees as the Middle Curvature Angle of Clavicle.

#### Precautions

- Ensure the clavicle lies flat and stable.
- Axes must be accurately aligned.
- Do not measure the surface curve; measure the angular deviation.
- Take two readings and record the average.

### 4. Lateral Curvature Angle

#### Instrument Required

- Goniometer

#### Anatomical Landmarks

- **Vertex of angle:** junction of middle and lateral third of clavicle
- **Arms of angle:**
  - Axis of middle third
  - Axis of lateral third

#### Procedure

1. Place the clavicle on a flat surface with the inferior surface downward.
2. Determine the maximum length of the clavicle.
3. Identify and mark:
   - the mid-point of the clavicle, and
   - the most lateral point of maximum curvature on the lateral third.
4. Draw or imagine:
   - a line along the axis of the middle segment, and
   - a line along the axis of the lateral segment (towards the acromial end).
5. Place the centre of the goniometer at the junction of the middle and lateral segments.
6. Measure the angle between the middle shaft axis and the lateral shaft axis.
7. Record the value in degrees as the Lateral Curvature Angle of Clavicle.

#### Precautions

- Ensure the clavicle lies completely flat.
- Axes must be properly aligned and not oblique.
- Measure the true angular deviation, not surface bending.
- Take two readings and record the average.
