MEASUREMENT 


	•	MAXIMUM LENGTH 

Instrument Required
	•	Osteometric board 
Anatomical Landmarks
	•	Medial landmark: Most projecting point of the sternal end
	•	Lateral landmark: Most projecting point of the acromial end

PROCEDURE 
	•	Place the clavicle on the osteometric board with its inferior surface resting on the board.
	•	Position the most medial point of the sternal end firmly against the fixed upright end of the board.
	•	Align the clavicle so that it lies straight along the board, despite its natural curvature.
	•	Slide the movable upright end of the board until it touches the most lateral projecting point of the acromial end.
	•	Read the measurement in millimeters.
	•	Record it as Maximum Length of Clavicle.


PRECAUTIONS
	•	Measure the straight length, not the curved surface.
	•	Ensure proper alignment of the bone.
	•	Avoid tilted or oblique positioning.
	•	Take two or three readings and record the average.


	•	MIDDLE SHAFT DIAMETER
Instrument Required
	•	Sliding calipers
Anatomical Landmarks
	•	Point: Mid-point of clavicular shaft
	•	Surfaces: Anterior and posterior borders at mid-shaft


PROCEDURE
	•	Place the clavicle on a flat surface.
	•	Identify the mid-point of the clavicular shaft:
	•	First determine the maximum length of the clavicle.
	•	Mark the exact midpoint of this length.
	•	At this midpoint, identify the antero-posterior margins of the shaft.
	•	Place the fixed jaw of the sliding caliper on the anterior surface of the clavicle.
	•	Place the movable jaw on the posterior surface.
	•	Hold the caliper perpendicular to the long axis of the clavicle.
	•	Read the measurement in millimeters.
	•	Record it as Middle Shaft Diameter of Clavicle.

PRECAUTIONS
	•	Ensure the midpoint is accurately located.
	•	Do not take an oblique measurement.
	•	Avoid excessive pressure with calipers.
	•	Take two readings and record the average.


	•	MIDDLE CURVATURE ANGLE

Instrument Required
	•	Goniometer
Anatomical Landmarks
	•	Vertex: Mid-point of clavicular shaft
	•	Arms of angle:
	•	Axis of medial half
	•	Axis of lateral half

PROCEDURE

	•	Place the clavicle on a flat horizontal surface with its inferior surface down.
	•	Determine the maximum length of the clavicle.
	•	Mark the mid-point of the shaft.
	•	Identify:
	•	the medial half axis of the clavicle (from sternal end to mid-point), and
	•	the lateral half axis (from mid-point to acromial end).
	•	Draw or imagine straight lines along both halves.
	•	Place the centre of the goniometer at the mid-point of the clavicle.
	•	Measure the angle formed between the medial and lateral axes.
	•	Record the value in degrees as the Middle Curvature Angle of Clavicle.

PRECAUTIONS
	•	Ensure the clavicle lies flat and stable.
	•	Axes must be accurately aligned.
	•	Do not measure the surface curve—measure the angular deviation.
	•	Take two readings and record the average.



	•	LATERAL CURVATURE ANGLE

Instrument Required

	•	Goniometer
Anatomical Landmarks
	•	Vertex of angle: Junction of middle and lateral third of clavicle
	•	Arms of angle:
	•	Axis of middle third
	•	Axis of lateral third

PROCEDURE
	•	Place the clavicle on a flat surface with the inferior surface downward.
	•	Determine the maximum length of the clavicle.
	•	Identify and mark:
	•	the mid-point of the clavicle, and
	•	the most lateral point of maximum curvature on the lateral third.
	•	Draw or imagine:
	•	a line along the axis of the middle segment, and
	•	a line along the axis of the lateral segment (towards the acromial end).
	•	Place the centre of the goniometer at the junction of the middle and lateral segments.
	•	Measure the angle between the middle shaft axis and the lateral shaft axis.
	•	Record the value in degrees as the Lateral Curvature Angle of Clavicle.



PRECAUTIONS
	•	Ensure the clavicle lies completely flat.
	•	Axes must be properly aligned and not oblique.
	•	Measure the true angular deviation, not surface bending.
	•	Take two readings and record the average.
